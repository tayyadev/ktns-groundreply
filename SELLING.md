# Sell KTNS GroundReply: a practical B2B offer

## Target buyer and problem

Initial buyer: an owner or support lead at a small SaaS company, IT support service, B2B software consultancy or specialty ecommerce operation that already has written FAQs and a human inbox, but whose employees repeatedly search documents and rewrite the same policy answers.

Offer a narrowly defined outcome: **"Your support team gets source-cited draft replies inside one review queue, without risking an uncontrolled AI auto-reply."** It is more believable than claiming a fully autonomous agent that resolves all tickets.

## Two commercial offers

| Package | Proposed price to test | Customer gets |
|---|---|---|
| Dedicated pilot | **$1,250–$3,000 one-time** | Installed branded workspace, up to 15 knowledge sources, setup/training, pilot QA, handoff. Hosting/model costs extra. |
| Managed subscription | **$79–$249/month (hypothesis)** | Hosted workspace, usage allowance, upgrades, basic support. Set actual Stripe price IDs only after unit-economics review. |

These prices are positioning hypotheses, **not** verified market rates or guaranteed sales. The included code permits multiple workspaces and optional Stripe recurring billing, but a real contract must define SLAs, model API usage, data rights, retention, refund terms, onboarding and support responsibilities.

## 6-minute product demonstration

1. **Problem:** Show a customer question asking for a return window, and explain how agents otherwise search docs and draft repeatedly.
2. **Knowledge:** Paste a permitted company policy (use `examples/refund-policy.txt` in the demo) and wait until indexed.
3. **Workflow:** Create the support ticket. Show category, source citations and the four-stage processing trace.
4. **Safety:** Ask an intentionally unsupported question (“Do you offer lifetime warranty?”). Show the abstention/escalation path rather than a fabricated answer.
5. **Human control:** Edit, approve and copy a drafted response.
6. **Engineering proof:** Open evaluation results, actor roles, team invitations and the audit trail.

A serious buyer will appreciate a visible guardrail and test failure more than a flashy unconditional completion animation.

## Differentiators against generic chatbots

- Knowledge sources explicitly displayed on each draft.
- Independent review step, deterministic citation ID checking, human approval.
- Separate workspace boundaries and auditable decisions.
- Regression tests and customer-created evaluation cases.
- Self-hosting/dedicated deployment available, not only a closed SaaS.

## Suggested direct prospecting filter

Target support teams where you can verify all three: (1) a public help center or FAQ exists, (2) live staff answer questions through email or ticketing, (3) someone with budget owns support operations. Avoid generic cold outreach to businesses with no support volume; the product needs a measurable repetitive-answer problem.

## Important sales disclosures

- No inbound Gmail, Zendesk, Intercom, Shopify, PDF ingestion or website chat widget is integrated in v0.1.
- Current delivery is human-approved manual copy/send. Don't advertise automatic ticket closure or unattended customer email.
- Models process submitted content through the configured provider; explain data handling and obtain customer approval.
- No claims of SOC 2, HIPAA, GDPR, 99.9% uptime, precise hallucination rate, automatic ROI or guaranteed response accuracy.
- The working name **KTNS GroundReply** requires legal/domain/trademark checking before broad public launch.

## Sales proof milestones

First establish one clean demo, then secure one **paid pilot** where the customer supplies permitted policy text and 20 historical support questions. Before expansion, measure response quality, average reviewer time saved, abstention rate, per-ticket token cost, and the customer's willingness to renew. Use real results and quotes only with the customer's permission.
